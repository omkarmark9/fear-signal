# v3: course correction after the 2026 holdout

v2 was frozen on 2023–2025 and run once on 2026 (`docs/RESULTS.md`). The
result was a validated fear thermometer, but with three weak spots:

* the new market index did not beat v1's on 2026 five-session changes (H1b);
* the tail forecast's calibration pass was fragile (H2, slope 0.801);
* there was no volatility increment over HAR (H4).

This document records how v3 was built from those results **without
fitting to them**, what changed, what was tried and rejected, and how v3 will
be judged.

## 1. The rule: 2026 is spent

Once a holdout has been looked at, anything chosen because of it is no
longer out-of-sample evidence. Every v3 decision therefore follows three
evidence tiers (`research/v3/overfitting-protocol/PROTOCOL.md`):

| tier | data | allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| A, confirmatory | sessions after 2026-11-23, unseen | the only out-of-sample evidence for v3: one pre-registered read |
| B, selection | 2023-07..2025-12 walk-forward, the same three folds as v2, refit per fold | the only data a choice may depend on |
| C, diagnostic | 2026 (spent), in-sample fits | explaining mechanisms; labelled post-hoc numbers; never a choice |

A change is adopted only if:

1. it was declared, with its mechanism, before scoring (at most four
   candidates per family, hashed in `research/v3/README.md`);
2. it beats the incumbent by at least one dependence-robust standard error on
   tier B;
3. it passes a data-snooping-robust family gate, Hansen's SPA_c with a
   prepivoted (double-bootstrap) level and Holm across families at a
   family-wise 10% (each threshold capped at 5%). A plain one-SE rule alone
   adopts a null candidate 47% of the time at this data's covariance. The
   gate's actual level on this data is about 6–7% at a nominal 5%, as
   measured on fresh seeds;
4. the gain fits its stated mechanism (per-fold signs, predicted
   signature).

Otherwise the outcome is **no change**.

## 2. How the round ran

Four diagnosis agents each took one weak spot: market aggregation, tail
calibration, volatility increment, and the overfitting protocol itself. An
adversarial critic then re-ran their key numbers and tried to reject every
proposal. Its verdicts and all raw outputs are in `research/v3/`. One further
family (F5) was declared afterwards, from a dev-visible finding of the
tail-calibration agent, and was scored under the same rule.

### Change v3-1: the market index on a linear yardstick

v2's walk-forward selected **logit breadth**, BL = logit(mean_i P_i(not calm)).
v2 then shipped a different object: its dev percentile, as a normal score,
which was never scored. Breadth is right-skewed, so that map is concave. It
stretches the dense calm region (gain 14 per unit of breadth below 0.2) and
compresses the mid-to-high-stress range (2.5–3 between 0.4 and 0.8), which is
exactly where VXN reacts most. v3 publishes the selected object on a fixed
linear scale:

```
M = (BL − μ) / σ        μ, σ: mean and s.d. of BL over the estimation period
```

The percentile score is kept as `M_pct` / `F_M`. Bands, alerts (`M_pct ≥ 2`)
and the high-conviction rule (`M_pct_lo ≥ 2`) stay on it. The change
preserves rank, so the alert days are identical to v2's.

### Change v3-2: how H4 is tested

HAR and HAR+M are re-estimated daily on an expanding window, using only
rows s ≤ t−5, whose 5-day targets are known at t. The models are nested
under a recursive scheme, so the test is Clark–West (2007). Diebold–Mariano
is undersized there. Feasible and oracle out-of-sample R² are shown side by
side. v2's frozen HAR failed 2026 through a level shift: 97% of its excess
MSE was the intercept, and its slopes were fine (Mincer–Zarnowitz slope
0.98). v2's published negative R² also used an oracle benchmark that already
knew 2026's mean.

### What the round learned that is not yet a change

* **Within-day over-dispersion of the tail forecast** (slope 0.50 on dev).
  One shared transition matrix treats an earnings-day drop as if it would
  persist like a crash. F5 addresses it and improves stock-level prediction,
  but at a cost to the market index. It is now a pre-registered challenger.
* **The 2026 tail-calibration dip is not new.** The dev folds already
  disagreed (slopes 1.31, 1.35, 0.65). The pooled 0.98 averaged a
  between-day over-response and a within-day over-dispersion that happened
  to cancel. 2026's 0.80 lies inside the fold range.
* **The emission design is at the resolution limit of the data.** At the dev
  covariance, a stock-layer gain needs about 0.003–0.004 nats/obs to be
  detected with 80% power under the family gate. v2's rejected alternatives
  differed by 0.0005–0.001.

## 5. Freezing and the prospective read

v3 is re-estimated by the same code on 2023-01-03..2026-11-23. This is
estimation, not selection: no choice depends on it. It is frozen in
`config/frozen_v3.json`. The F5 challenger is frozen on the same window in
`config/frozen_v3_challenger_F5.json`. The tests are pre-registered in
`docs/PREREGISTRATION_v3.md`. They are read **once**, at exactly the first 400
sessions after 2026-11-23 (about 2028-06-28). The code refuses to load that
sample earlier, or before the specs are committed, and refuses a second read
without a logged reason. v2 (frozen) and v1 (published) are re-read on the
same sample as comparators.
